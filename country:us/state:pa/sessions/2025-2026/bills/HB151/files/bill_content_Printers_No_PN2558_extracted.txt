MACKENZIE, MADDEN, BURGOS, CARROLL, HOWARD, RIVERA, STEELE, CIRESI, SMITH-WADE-EL, SHUSTERMAN, FRIEL AND CEPEDA-FREYTIZ, NOVEMBER 6, 2025 REFERRED TO COMMITTEE ON CHILDREN AND YOUTH, NOVEMBER 6, 2025 AN ACTAmending the act of November 23, 2010 (P.L.1264, No.119), entitled "An act providing for requirements for children in foster care and for grievance policy and procedure," further providing for definitions; and providing for benefits.The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania hereby enacts as follows:Section 1. The definitions of "county agency," "department" and "private agency" in section 2 of the act of November 23, 2010 (P.L.1264, No.119), known as the Children in Foster Care Act, are amended and the section is amended by adding definitions to read:Section 2. Definitions.The following words and phrases when used in this act shall have the meanings given to them in this section unless the context clearly indicates otherwise:"Benefits." Any and all Federal benefits, including Social Security, Supplemental Security Income and veterans benefits.1234567891011121314151617 * * *"Cost of care." The reasonable maintenance expenses, including foster care maintenance payments as defined under 42 U.S.C. § 675(4)(A) (relating to definitions), and special allowances for a child of similar age."County agency." A county children and youth social service agency established under section 405 of the act of June 24, 1937 (P.L.2017, No.396), known as the County Institution District Law, and supervised by the Department of [Public Welfare] Human Services under Article IX of the act of June 13, 1967 (P.L.31, No.21), known as the [Public Welfare] Human Services Code."Department." The Department of [Public Welfare] Human Services of the Commonwealth.* * *"Private agency." A children and youth social service agency subject to the regulatory requirements of the Department of [Public Welfare] Human Services.* * *"Representative payee." A person designated to receive benefits for a minor child under county agency rules governing the benefits.* * *"Unmet needs." Expenses for which a county agency is not required by law to provide financial support.* * *Section 2. The act is amended by adding a section to read:Section 5.1. Benefits.A county agency shall have the following powers and duties:(1) The county agency shall ensure that each child in foster care is screened to determine whether the child is 20250HB0151PN2558 - 2 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 currently receiving or is eligible to receive Social Security, Supplemental Security Income, veterans or other Federal or State benefits within 60 days of being placed in foster care and annually thereafter.(2) Within 60 days of a determination that a child is potentially eligible to receive Social Security, Supplemental Security Income, veterans or other Federal or State benefits, the county agency shall apply for the benefits, in cooperation with the child's attorney or guardian ad litem, if the screening determines that the child may be eligible for benefits.(3) If an application for benefits is denied, the county agency shall appeal the decision.(4) The county agency shall identify, in cooperation with the child's attorney or guardian ad litem, a representative payee in accordance with the established categories of preferred payees as outlined by Social Security and Supplemental Security Income guidelines and apply to become the representative payee if there is no other suitable candidate available.(5) The county agency shall support the maintenance of benefits for a child transitioning out of foster care or an individual under the county agency's care who has reached the age of legal adulthood. When a child in foster care reaches 16 years and 6 months of age, the county agency shall begin to prepare a plan to support the maintenance of benefits for the child as the child transitions out of foster care or reaches the age of legal adulthood. If a child's eligibility for benefits ceases upon entering foster care, the county agency shall assist the child and parent, guardian, permanent 20250HB0151PN2558 - 3 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 legal custodian or adoptive parent with the application for benefits upon the child transitioning out of foster care.(6) Notwithstanding 55 Pa. Code Ch. 3140 (relating to planning and financial reimbursement requirements for county children and youth social service programs), if the county agency serves as a representative payee for a child receiving Social Security, Supplemental Security Income, veterans or other Federal or State benefits, the county agency shall:(i) Use or conserve the benefits in the child's interest, as determined in cooperation with the child and the child's attorney or guardian ad litem, including using the benefits for services or unmet needs not otherwise provided by the county agency, court ordered or covered by health insurance or conserving the benefits for the child's reasonably foreseeable future needs.(ii) Ensure that for a child in foster care, the child's benefits are not used to reimburse the Commonwealth for the costs of care for the child or other payments made by the Commonwealth to cover any other cost or expense for the child.(iii) Monitor any Federal asset or resource limit for the benefits and ensure that a child's best interest is served by using or conserving the benefits in a way that avoids violating the Federal asset or resource limit, including conserving funds by establishing individual financial accounts in accordance with 20 CFR 416.645 (relating to conservation and investment of benefit payments).(7) Consistent with the best interest of a child in foster care, the county agency shall place the child's 20250HB0151PN2558 - 4 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 benefits in individual financial accounts that avoid asset limitations for Federal and State programs, such as a PA ABLE savings account. The county agency shall establish individual financial accounts for the purpose of limiting the county agency's use of a child's benefits. The county agency shall cooperate with the Social Security Administration, the child and the child's attorney or guardian ad litem to ensure that when the child leaves foster care or reaches 18 years of age, all payments of benefits shall be returned to the Social Security Administration to be held on the child's behalf or, upon the instructions of the Social Security Administration, transferred to the child or a new representative payee.(8) The county agency shall notify the parent, guardian, permanent legal custodian or adoptive parent and child through the child's attorney or guardian ad litem of an application, decision, communication or appeal related to the child's Social Security, Supplemental Security Income, veterans or other Federal or State benefits.(9) The county agency shall provide a quarterly accounting of the amount and use of all benefits that each child in foster care is receiving. The accounting shall contain an itemized list of each expenditure by the county agency, private agency and any third-party representative payee. The accounting shall be provided to the child through the child's attorney or guardian ad litem.(10) The county agency shall provide a child in foster care with ongoing financial literacy training tailored to the individual child's development, functioning and needs and support in maintaining benefit eligibility, including providing information regarding the existence, availability 20250HB0151PN2558 - 5 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 and use of money conserved for the child. The training and support shall be provided when a child in foster care reaches 14 years of age or within six months of being placed in foster care for children over 14 years of age. Financial literacy training concerning restrictions on the use of conserved money shall be made available to all parents, guardians, permanent legal custodians and adoptive parents gaining access to money conserved by the county agency.(11) The department and county agency shall assess the feasibility of providing repayments for a child formerly in foster care whose benefits were not used in the best interest of the child.(12) The county agency shall collect, maintain and provide data relating to the application, use and conservation of Federal and State benefits for a child in foster care in an annual report provided to the public. The report may not reveal to the public identifying information about a child either currently or formerly in foster care.(13) Conservation of the benefits shall fulfill the exhaustion of eligibility and receipt of benefits requirements under section 704.2 of the act of June 13, 1967 (P.L.31, No.21), known as the Human Services Code.(14) The department and county agency may contract with a third party to fulfill any of the requirements under this section.(15) Within nine months of the effective date of this section, the Department of Human Services Office for Children, Youth and Families shall issue guidance on the implementation of this section. The following shall apply:(i) Not later than 30 days after the effective date 20250HB0151PN2558 - 6 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 of this section, the office shall convene a meeting with stakeholders who shall include, at a minimum, representatives from county welfare directors, child advocacy organizations, current and former foster children and other relevant stakeholders, as determined by the department.(ii) The guidance shall include best practice guidelines for county child welfare agencies. Best practice guidelines shall include establishing procedures for the following:(A) Determining the manner and tools for conducting disability screenings for children who may be eligible for benefits.(B) Assisting in the application process for benefits for each child who, pursuant to the disability screening, is likely to be determined eligible for benefits.(C) Requesting reconsideration and appealing adverse decisions where appropriate.(D) Informing parents and caretakers at the time a child leaves foster care of potential eligibility for benefits for the child if the child is not receiving benefits but may be eligible, upon application, to receive those benefits.(E) Informing foster children of their rights and responsibilities for the continued receipt of benefits, the sources of assistance that may be available for resolving problems foster children may have with the receipt of benefits and the process for transferring accumulated benefits.20250HB0151PN2558 - 7 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 (F) Providing counties strategies for leveraging existing providers of civil legal services and leveraging funds under 42 U.S.C. Ch. 7 Subch. IV Pt. E ( relating to Federal Payments for Foster Care, Prevention and Permanency) to provide legal assistance to children and youth to comply with the law.Section 3. This act shall take effect in 60 days.20250HB0151PN2558 - 8 - 12345678
