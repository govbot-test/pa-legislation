A RESOLUTION
Directing the Joint State Government Commission to study the
impact of private equity in child care and make
recommendations for limiting negative effects.
WHEREAS, Child care staffing recruitment has proven difficult
due to inadequate pay, resulting in a decreased supply of child
care in recent years, despite steady demand; and
WHEREAS, With the high costs of child care, parents are
unable to pay more than they are currently spending; and
WHEREAS, As independent and nonprofit child care providers
struggle to maintain their programs due to financial hardships,
programs supported by private, for-profit companies are making
significantly higher profits while consistently increasing fees
for parents; and
WHEREAS, As private equity firms partner with the child care
industry, the firms are able to make a profit through serving
middle, upper-middle and affluent families, allowing them to
increase the cost of child care while prioritizing enrollment
and reducing operational costs and maintaining low staff

WHEREAS, Another tactic used by private equity firms is
receiving the proceeds from a child care center selling its
property, leading to the child care center leasing the property

debt while the private equity firm receives the proceeds; and
WHEREAS, Additionally, child care providers across the nation
are reporting higher liability insurance costs, reduced coverage
and policies being dropped by insurance companies; and
WHEREAS, Liability insurance is necessary for child care
providers to protect themselves from financial loss, lawsuits,
injuries and accidents; and
WHEREAS, Furthermore, when child care centers have to pay
more for insurance, these costs are often passed on to parents
through increased tuition; and
WHEREAS, Without liability insurance, child care providers
take on the risk of losing their license, closing their business
or serving fewer families; and
WHEREAS, As liability insurance is increasing, child care
centers have to determine whether to pay for the rising
insurance costs, close their business or risk operating without
it; and
WHEREAS, It is important that the impact of private equity
firms operating in the child care industry is analyzed and
understood in order to ensure that the best interests of
children are prioritized and parents are provided affordable
child care; therefore be it
RESOLVED, That the House of Representatives direct the Joint
State Government Commission to study the impact of private
equity in child care and make recommendations for limiting
