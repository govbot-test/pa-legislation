2025-2026 Regular Session HR 363 PN 2571 Bill Text (HTM) See other bills under thesame topic PRINTER'S NO. 2571 THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY OF PENNSYLVANIAHOUSE RESOLUTION No.363 Session of 2025 INTRODUCED BY SHUSTERMAN, HILL-EVANS, WAXMAN, HANBIDGE, FREEMAN, MERSKI, OTTEN, SANCHEZ, HOWARD AND GREEN, OCTOBER 31, 2025 REFERRED TO COMMITTEE ON CHILDREN AND YOUTH, NOVEMBER 6, 2025 A RESOLUTIONDirecting the Joint State Government Commission to study the impact of private equity in child care and make recommendations for limiting negative effects.WHEREAS, Child care staffing recruitment has proven difficult due to inadequate pay, resulting in a decreased supply of child care in recent years, despite steady demand; andWHEREAS, With the high costs of child care, parents are unable to pay more than they are currently spending; andWHEREAS, As independent and nonprofit child care providers struggle to maintain their programs due to financial hardships, programs supported by private, for-profit companies are making significantly higher profits while consistently increasing fees for parents; andWHEREAS, As private equity firms partner with the child care industry, the firms are able to make a profit through serving middle, upper-middle and affluent families, allowing them to increase the cost of child care while prioritizing enrollment and reducing operational costs and maintaining low staff 123456789101112131415161718 salaries, causing high staff turnover rates; andWHEREAS, Another tactic used by private equity firms is receiving the proceeds from a child care center selling its property, leading to the child care center leasing the property from the private equity firm, leaving the child care center in debt while the private equity firm receives the proceeds; andWHEREAS, Additionally, child care providers across the nation are reporting higher liability insurance costs, reduced coverage and policies being dropped by insurance companies; andWHEREAS, Liability insurance is necessary for child care providers to protect themselves from financial loss, lawsuits, injuries and accidents; andWHEREAS, Furthermore, when child care centers have to pay more for insurance, these costs are often passed on to parents through increased tuition; andWHEREAS, Without liability insurance, child care providers take on the risk of losing their license, closing their business or serving fewer families; andWHEREAS, As liability insurance is increasing, child care centers have to determine whether to pay for the rising insurance costs, close their business or risk operating without it; andWHEREAS, It is important that the impact of private equity firms operating in the child care industry is analyzed and understood in order to ensure that the best interests of children are prioritized and parents are provided affordable child care; therefore be itRESOLVED, That the House of Representatives direct the Joint State Government Commission to study the impact of private equity in child care and make recommendations for limiting 20250HR0363PN2571 - 2 - 123456789101112131415161718192021222324252627282930 negative effects; and be it furtherRESOLVED, That the Joint State Government Commission study the reasons for and the impact of rising liability insurance rates on child care facilities; and be it furtherRESOLVED, That the Joint State Government Commission examine actions taken by other states to evaluate the outcomes of private equity firms involved with the child care industry and limit negative effects of this partnership, along with how other states are addressing liability insurance for child care facilities to mitigate risks; and be it furtherRESOLVED, That the Joint State Government Commission issue a report with its findings and legislative recommendations to the House of Representatives within 12 months of the adoption of this resolution.20250HR0363PN2571 - 3 - 1234567891011121314
