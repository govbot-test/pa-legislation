A RESOLUTION
Recognizing November 1, 2025, as "Tylenol Day" in Pennsylvania.
WHEREAS, In 1884, two German physicians prescribed
naphthalene to a patient they were treating for worms and a
fever; and
WHEREAS, The naphthalene did not relieve the patient's worms,
but it did reduce the patient's fever, an effect that was not
previously known; and
WHEREAS, The physicians learned that the pharmacy had made a
mistake and provided acetanilide, a compound isolated from coal
tar; and
WHEREAS, The physicians worked with a pharmaceutical firm to
investigate the effects of acetanilide and confirmed that it
reduced fever; and
WHEREAS, After acetanilide was marketed as Antifebrin, users
reported bluish skin after taking it; and
WHEREAS, The cause of the bluish skin was identified as
methemoglobinemia, a condition in which the oxygen transported

WHEREAS, These findings led researchers to seek safer
alternatives by altering the molecular structure of compounds to
eliminate side effects; and
WHEREAS, In 1887, a German company known as Bayer modified
acetanilide to produce phenacetin, which did not cause
methemoglobinemia; and
WHEREAS, Later in 1887, a physician and a chemist worked
together to find another derivative of acetanilide, now known as
acetaminophen; and
WHEREAS, The founders of acetaminophen recommended an
extensive investigation into the medication before it became
available to the public; and
WHEREAS, The effectiveness and safety of acetaminophen as a
fever and pain reliever were not confirmed until 1949, based on
modern research techniques and clinical use in England; and
WHEREAS, Because phenacetin was linked to kidney problems and
aspirin to stomach problems, acetaminophen was promoted as the
safer medication; and
WHEREAS, In the late 1940s, McNeil Laboratories, a
Philadelphia family-owned business, began studying the safety
and efficacy of acetaminophen in hopes of marketing it for a
future product; and
WHEREAS, McNeil Laboratories began as a family-run pharmacy
and later expanded into the manufacturing of prescription
medicines; and
WHEREAS, In 1955, McNeil Laboratories introduced Tylenol
Elixir for Children after receiving approval by the United
States Food and Drug Administration (FDA); and
WHEREAS, Tylenol Elixir for Children was the first single-

WHEREAS, In 1959, Johnson & Johnson acquired McNeil
Laboratories; and
WHEREAS, In 1959, Tylenol was approved for sale without a
prescription, leading to its availability over the counter the
following year; and
WHEREAS, Tylenol has become the best-known product of the
more than 600 products on the market that contain the active
ingredient acetaminophen; and
WHEREAS, Tylenol has more than two dozen products in various
forms, including liquids, dissolvable powders, chewable tablets,
caplets and capsules; and
WHEREAS, Each Tylenol product is formulated for different
symptoms, age groups and delivery methods; and
WHEREAS, Tylenol offers products for pain and fever relief,
as well as for arthritis, cold and flu symptoms, sinus relief
and sleep; and
WHEREAS, It is estimated that nearly 25% of adults in the
United States use a medication containing acetaminophen each
week; and
WHEREAS, Tylenol is the number one doctor-recommended pain
reliever brand; and
WHEREAS, The year 2025 commemorates the 70th anniversary of
Tylenol receiving FDA approval and being introduced by McNeil
Laboratories; therefore be it
RESOLVED, That the House of Representatives recognize
November 1, 2025, as "Tylenol Day" in Pennsylvania.
